\notes{\subsection{The Crooks in GAIST is a Different Paper}}

\notes{The name Crooks in [@Welling-generative26] refers to the *fluctuation theorem* [@Crooks-fluctuation99], not to thermodynamic length. Chapter 17 derives
$$
\frac{P(W)}{P_R(-W)} = e^{\beta(W-\Delta F)}
$$
and obtains Jarzynski's equality $\langle e^{-\beta W}\rangle = e^{-\beta\Delta F}$ by integration, hence $\langle W\rangle \ge \Delta F$. That is a second-law statement about work and free-energy differences, averaged over trajectories. It is not a length on the equilibrium manifold, and it is not $\langle W_{\mathrm{ex}}\rangle \ge \mathcal{L}^2/\tau$.}

\notes{GAIST does not define thermodynamic length, does not introduce the Fisher--Rao metric on a family of equilibrium states, and does not cite Crooks (2007). Fisher information appears once, in Section 22.6, as the osmotic term $\nabla\log\rho$ in the Schrödinger-bridge action --- a different object from the Fisher matrix $g_{ij}$ on the board today.}
