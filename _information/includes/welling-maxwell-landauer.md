\notes{\subsection{GAIST on Maxwell and Landauer}}

\notes{Section 3.3 of [@Welling-generative26], pages 49--52, is the book's account of today's pair. Heat is energy stored in fluctuations we cannot couple to; work is energy stored in a few accessible macroscopic degrees of freedom. The split is subjective: it depends on the devices we can build. Maxwell's demon is the being who *can* couple to the molecules, and so appears to extract work from a single bath.}

\notes{The resolution is Landauer and Bennett [@Landauer-irreversibility61;@Bennett-thermodynamics82]: the demon must acquire, store, and erase information, and that process increases entropy by at least as much as the system lost. The modern review is @Parrondo-thermodynamics15. GAIST does not develop Szilard's engine, and does not yet state the $k_B T\ln 2$ bound as a calculation. Read Landauer's two pages for that. The finite-time correction to Landauer --- you cannot saturate $k_B T\ln 2$ in finite time --- is Section 22.3, and waits until week 8.}
