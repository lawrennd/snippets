\notes{In the *Financial Times* I argued that attempting to measure human capital poses a productivity paradox. The piece is the shortest statement of the next two beats.}

\speakernotes{Show the FT screenshot briefly — venue-friendly — then move into inflation and the qualitative remainder. Do not reopen the full attention-flywheel lecture.}

\newslide{Inflation of Human Capital}

\slidesincremental{
* Automating mental labour is like discovering the philosopher's stone
* Efficiency rises; measured skills inflate and lose scarcity value
* A supply-side reflex chases what the machine can already quantify
}

\notes{Automating mental labour is the digital philosopher's stone. Combine silicon with electrons and you complete tasks faster than a human can. That creates efficiency. It also triggers inflation of human capital. Skills that can be measured, scored and standardised lose scarcity value as the machine absorbs them. Europe's lead on indices of education and health is real — and double-edged if we treat those measured stocks as the whole of the prize.}

\newslide{What Does Not Inflate}

\slidesincremental{
* What the machine can quantify, it can automate
* What matters most is hardest to measure: judgment, care, trust
* That work lives on the demand side, next to real problems
}

\notes{There is a qualitative–quantitative separation. Machines rely on measurable outputs. Anything we can put on a dashboard is at risk of automation. The atomic remainder — the nurse's extra minutes, the officer's local judgment, the teacher's encouragement of a struggling student — is hard to quantify and therefore hard to replace. Hayek's point in *The Use of Knowledge in Society* still holds: the knowledge that matters for good decisions is dispersed among people on the ground, and cannot be fully aggregated into a central plan or a set of metrics [@Hayek-knowledge45]. Supply-side policy is comfortable with what shows up in compute targets and model benchmarks. The productivity prize sits where those measures fail: adoption, institutions, and the people closest to the problem. That is why the demand side is not a soft afterthought. It is where non-inflating human capital is put to work.}
