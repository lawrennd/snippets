\newslide{A mistake markets make}

\slidesincremental{
* Markets often treat AI as one centralised deployment
* Value arrives *workflow by workflow*, with people who know the work
* “Centralised deployment” is a supplier story; useful deployment is a demand story
}

\notes{In a [letter to the *Financial Times*](https://www.ft.com/content/75ebaeb0-e250-4030-93b9-25fb517109ca)
(12 May 2026), I replied to reporting on Visma’s postponed IPO and to the
question whether a group of some 170 businesses can “create the operational
change centrally” for AI. That premise is wrong. AI is not one thing applied
once from the centre. A payroll team in Oslo and a tax team in Lisbon face
different regulations, customers and data. Federated structures, properly
supported, sit closer to where that value is made — not further from it.}

\newslide{Implication for software groups}

\slidesincremental{
* Do not value a portfolio as if AI were a single centre-led programme
* Underwrite the *local* judgment layer in each workflow
* Support demand-side adoption — do not only sell a supplier story
}

\notes{For software CEOs, founders and investors, the letter is a diligence
filter. Seat counts and model features are easy to aggregate. Workflow-level
adoption, with explicit judgment and recovery paths, is harder — and that is
where durable value sits. The same confusion appears in policy when compute
and frontier models are privileged over how people adopt tools inside their
own institutions.}
